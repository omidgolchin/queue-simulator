How the recommendation is made formulas, simulation, sources

Erlang C gives the chance a caller waits and the wait distribution when calls arrive at random, handle times are exponential and nobody hangs up (Gans, Koole & Mandelbaum, "Telephone call centers: tutorial, review, and research prospects", Manufacturing & Service Operations Management, 2003). The recommendation is the fewest agents whose service level meets your target.

offered load a = calls per minute × handle time (minutes) P(wait) = Erlang C(c, a) service level = 1 − P(wait) · exp(−(c − a) · T / handle time) average wait (ASA) = P(wait) · handle time / (c − a) occupancy = a / c

Occupancy cap. If the target is met with agents busier than the cap, agents are added until they are not. Sustained occupancy above about 90% raises handle times and turnover, so the formula's answer is not schedulable.

Callers who hang up. Erlang C assumes infinite patience and overstates waits when callers abandon. With a patience set, the recommendation comes from a simulation of the queue with exponential patience (20 independent runs of 8 hours after a 1-hour warm-up, the same random numbers for every agent count), and the service level counts hung-up calls as missed. The simulation engine agrees with this repository's SimPy simulator within its confidence intervals (tested).

Shrinkage converts agents on the phones into people to schedule: scheduled = agents ÷ (1 − shrinkage), rounded up.

The experiments behind this tool (validation against Erlang C and Little's law, the rush-hour finding, whether language models can do queueing math) are in Research notes.

How the day plan is made interval staffing

Each hour is staffed on its own with Erlang C, the standard practice in workforce management: a day staffed for its average hour leaves the rush badly understaffed (in this repository's test, 41% of callers waited over 20 seconds at the peak). The day's shape is either a typical weekday (the hourly pattern of NYC 311 calls, January 2024, scaled to your daily total), flat, or your own counts.

The day plan assumes callers do not hang up, which makes it slightly generous. Use Plan one hour with a patience set to check a busy hour by simulation.
